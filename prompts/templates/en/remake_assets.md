Watch the complete video and build a small, accurate, globally deduplicated visual asset library. Internally count appearances; include only assets visibly present in at least two independent shots or discontinuous time spans. An uninterrupted appearance counts once. Mentions in dialogue, subtitles or promotional text do not count. Changes of angle, action, lighting or temporary state do not create new assets. Do not output counts or reasoning.

Write names and descriptions in English, preserving established proper names. description contains the asset's stable visual details, not turnaround, four-panel or aspect-ratio instructions; the renderer adds layouts. Be specific and distinctive, but omit current actions, temporary expressions and framing. Do not invent properties the video cannot establish.

Characters include humans, animals and groups. Set label to person, animal or group, describing form rather than importance.
For an individual human or animal, use exactly these 14 rows in order: Era; Country/Dynasty; Ethnicity/Species; World type; Face; Hair/Fur; Ear accessories; Build; Head-to-body ratio; Upper clothing; Lower clothing; Footwear; Gender; Age. Write each as **Label**: value on its own line. Cover stable face/body features and clothing. For animals, describe species, head/muzzle/ears, fur/feathers/scales, body proportions, equipment and feet. Describe colors, materials, structure, patterns, wear and layers. Retain every row; use unknown for details not visible. Do not infer unseen identity or appearance.
For groups use exactly these 15 rows: Group identity; Age range; Gender; Ethnicity/Species; Group size; Build; Face; Eyebrows; Eyewear; Nose; Lips; Skin; Distinctive marks; Hair/Fur; Clothing and props. Give shared features first, then supported individual variation. Do not impose one member's incidental feature on everyone or describe temporary formations/actions. Retain unidentifiable fields explicitly.

Create locations only for genuinely different places; merge angles, shot sizes and subregions. Use a continuous spatial description, not a field list:
1. Wide horizontal panorama, realistic scale and planar perspective; straight lines and horizon, no fisheye, spherical, barrel or tiny-planet distortion.
2. Location name, period style, indoor/outdoor setting, scale, shape and main axis.
3. Ground, architecture, terrain and permanent fixtures from foreground to background, with precise positions and relationships.
4. Counts, spacing, directions and extents of repeated or symmetric elements, keeping topology consistent across views.
5. Viewpoint, direction, horizon height, stable light sources, weather and palette.
Exclude people, animals, temporary props, actions, combat, abilities and effects from location descriptions. Permanent architectural fixtures remain part of the scene. Avoid spatially ambiguous words such as nearby or several.

Keep only repeatedly visible key props requiring consistent appearance. In one coherent paragraph describe category, silhouette, proportions, materials, colors, components, patterns or legible writing, texture, wear and distinctive features. Merge angles, open/closed states and minor recognition differences of the same item.
Ignore passersby, ordinary decoration, one-off groups, places and props, and irrelevant detail. Names must be short and stable.
